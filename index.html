Barkley Slope El Nino Watch

Ocean Networks Canada cabled records, southwest Vancouver Island. Status as of 2026-11-07 (data to 7 November 2026). Tropical index: ONI (ASO 2026) about +2.5; RONI +1.97.

The 2026 El Nino is reaching Vancouver Island through the ocean, not the air. The surface stays near normal - cold, even - while the Barkley slope at 396 m turns warmer, fresher, lighter and more oxygen-rich all at once: the fingerprint of density surfaces pushed down about 50-100 m by a coastally trapped wave from the tropics. Coastal sea level rose +6 to +8 cm as the wave passed. The same response, smaller, appeared in 2023 - so it is repeatable and can be anticipated.

The story in one figure

One timeline: the tropical El Nino is huge (A); the surface off Vancouver Island saw little, even cold, in autumn (B - a NOAA buoy and ONC 25 m agree, r = 0.91); 396 m had its warmest month in 17 years (C); oxygen at depth is rising (D). Invisible at the surface, record-breaking at depth.

How this complements the ONC monitor

Steve Mihaly's ONC slope & canyon monitor tracks the records as they break, across more depths (to 2658 m). This page adds the why: the coastally trapped-wave mechanism, independent coastal sea-level evidence, the heave-versus-deoxygenation split on density surfaces, the 2023 repeatability case, and a forecast for 2027.

What to expect

  • 2026 is the onset. The main response off BC usually peaks in winter (Dec-Mar) - the signal should grow.
  • If the 26.6 density surface stays deep into spring, water upwelled onto the shelf in 2027 will be warmer, fresher and more oxygen-rich than usual.
  • The slope should cool after the El Nino peaks, as it did after 2015-16 and 2023-24.
